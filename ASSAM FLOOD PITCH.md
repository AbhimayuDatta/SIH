# Pitch Brief: A Data-Driven Flood Resilience Initiative for Assam

## The Problem

Assam floods almost every year — and the damage keeps growing. Districts like Barpeta, Goalpara, Nalbari, and Silchar (Cachar) are hit hardest, with housing loss, mass displacement, and destroyed cropland accounting for nearly **40% of all flood damage** in the state. Most of this damage concentrates into a brutal **one-month window (mid-July to mid-August)** each year — a predictable pattern that current disaster response still treats as an emergency, not a plan.

The deeper problem: Assam's flood strategy is **reactive and infrastructure-only**. Embankments are the default defense, but over 80% are under-maintained, and when they breach, the "levee effect" means communities that trusted the wall are hit _harder_, not softer. Meanwhile, natural buffers — the wetlands that used to absorb excess water — have quietly disappeared (one wetland in Barpeta, Korila Bil, has nearly vanished since 1955).

## Why It Matters Now

- **~30 million people** in India are affected by floods annually; Assam is one of the most exposed states.
- Damage is estimated at **INR 10,000–15,000 crore (~USD 1.2–1.8B) per decade** — and rising with climate-driven rainfall intensity.
- The tools to fix this already exist and are proven: GIS-based susceptibility mapping (Cachar study) and empirical damage-index modeling (statewide ASDMA data) both show _exactly_ where and when the risk concentrates. That intelligence isn't being operationalized.

## Root Cause, in One Line

Rainfall triggers floods — but **embankment neglect, wetland loss, and low-income community exposure** decide how much damage actually happens. Damage is not a weather problem; it's a preparedness and infrastructure-maintenance problem.

## The Opportunity

Combine two things Assam already has — (1) district-level flood susceptibility maps and (2) daily ASDMA damage-reporting data — into a **single predictive, pre-emptive resource-allocation system**:

1. **Hotspot targeting** — Rank districts/blocks by a live Flood Damage Index, not just rainfall forecasts, so relief and maintenance budgets go where realized damage is historically highest (Barpeta, Goalpara, Nalbari, Silchar/Katigorah).
2. **Pre-monsoon embankment triage** — Use breach-history data to prioritize inspection and repair _before_ the mid-July window, not after a breach.
3. **Wetland restoration as flood infrastructure** — Treat swamp/wetland reclamation as a line-item flood defense, not just an environmental nice-to-have, given its measurable inverse correlation with damage.
4. **Community-level early warning + resilient housing pilots** — Pair top-down data with the indigenous adaptive practices already working in villages like Gerjai (Dhemaji) — raised platforms, community rescue networks, altered cropping patterns — and scale what's already effective.

## Who Benefits

- **State disaster management authorities (ASDMA)** — better-targeted budgets and pre-season action windows instead of reactive relief.
- **~1.7M+ residents of high-risk districts** (e.g., Cachar alone) — reduced housing loss and displacement.
- **Smallholder farmers** — protection for the ~10% of damage currently coming from crop loss.
- **NGOs and donors** — a transparent, data-backed way to direct funding to measurably high-impact interventions.

## The Ask

Support to build and pilot a **district-level Flood Risk Dashboard** integrating GIS susceptibility scores, live ASDMA damage reporting, and embankment-condition tracking — piloted in the two highest-need districts (Barpeta and Cachar) ahead of the next monsoon season, with a community early-warning component modeled on existing indigenous practices.
